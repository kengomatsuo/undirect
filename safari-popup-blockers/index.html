Safari pop-up and redirect blockers compared

Seven ways to stop pages opening ads, tabs and redirects in Safari on iPhone, iPad and Mac. We make Undirect, one of the seven, so every fact about the others comes from Apple's pages or the app's own App Store listing, read on 20 November 2026, and each entry says where it beats Undirect.

Prices and ratings are what each App Store listing showed in dollars on 20 November 2026. They differ from one country's store to another and they change, so check the listing before you buy. We have not run the six others side by side on the same pages; this is what their makers say they do.

Which one fits

  • If ads, banners, trackers or cookie notices are the problem, use a content blocker: 1Blocker, AdGuard or Ghostery. All three are free to start.
  • If sites keep opening new tabs, start with a free tab blocker, TabGuard or Tab Blocker, or use Undirect.
  • If your taps land on ad pages through a layer you cannot see, or links change under your finger, use Undirect. Of the seven listings, it is the only one that says it removes an invisible layer before your tap lands and puts back a link rewritten mid-tap.
  • If you want nothing installed, turn on Safari's own settings, which stop pop-up windows a page opens without a tap.

A content blocker and a tab or tap guard do different jobs, so you can run one of each.

Safari's own settings

Block Pop-ups on iPhone and iPad (Settings, Apps, Safari) and Pop-up Windows on a Mac (Safari, Settings, Websites) stop windows a page opens without your tap. On a Mac you set it per website: Allow, Block, or Block and Notify. Fraudulent Website Warning warns you when you visit a fraudulent site. Apple's pop-up article also tells you to close a tab instead of tapping a close button that may be fake.

Where it beats Undirect

It costs nothing and is already installed. Block and Notify on a Mac lets you open a pop-up you wanted from the address bar.

A window opened on your tap counts as one you asked for, so it gets through; why Safari still opens ads with Block Pop-ups on explains how pages use that.

1Blocker

1Blocker is a content blocker for iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Vision Pro, made since 2015. It gives Safari filter lists for ads, more than 50,000 trackers, annoyances such as cookie notices, marketing widgets and chat pop-ups, and adult sites, and Safari does the blocking; the listing says the app has no access to web pages. It is free with one category on at a time, such as Block Ads. Premium, which adds custom rules, costs $2.99 a month, $14.99 a year or $39.99 once, and one purchase covers every device (App Store, 1blocker.com).

Where it beats Undirect

It blocks ads and trackers, which Undirect leaves alone, it is free to start, it runs on iOS 16, and it has 13,137 ratings averaging 4.5. Its listing is open about its limit: "not all ads can be blocked", because some sites use techniques Safari content blockers cannot reach. Undirect vs 1Blocker goes further.

AdGuard

AdGuard Ad Blocker for Safari is a content blocker for iPhone and iPad with more than 50 filter lists to choose from, and its DNS filtering also blocks requests outside Safari. Its listing names banners, pop-ups, autoplay videos and ads, YouTube included, plus tracking scripts. Safari blocking is free with no account; Pro adds advanced YouTube blocking, DNS protection for apps, custom rules and security filters, with a 7-day trial, and the listing shows purchases from $1.99 to $65.99. On a Mac it is a separate app, AdGuard Mini, also free with a Pro upgrade (App Store, adguard.com).

Where it beats Undirect

Free ad and tracker blocking, filtering in other apps through DNS, support back to iOS 15, open source code, and 20,537 ratings averaging 4.4. Undirect vs AdGuard goes further.

Ghostery

Ghostery's Safari app, Ghostery AdBlocker for Privacy, runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac. Its listing says it blocks ads and pop-ups on all websites, YouTube and Facebook included, stops trackers, removes cookie consent pop-ups by itself and shows which trackers each site runs. It is free and open source; the in-app purchases are optional contributions (App Store).

Where it beats Undirect

It is free, it clears cookie consent pop-ups, which Undirect does not touch, and it shows you who is tracking you on each site. It needs iOS 18.7 or macOS 14.6 and has 214 ratings averaging 4.3.

TabGuard

TabGuard is a Safari extension built for tabs. Its listing says it blocks pop-up windows opened with window.open(), links that force a new tab, pop-unders and "sneaky redirect tricks". It has three modes: Block All; Smart Mode, which allows your clicks and blocks tabs a script opens; and Allow All. You can allow or block single sites, and it shows a notice on the page and keeps a history of what it blocked. Everything runs on the device (App Store).

Where it beats Undirect

It is free and runs on iOS 17. Block All stops every new tab a site tries, which suits a site where you never want one. It is at version 1.0 from December 2025, in English only, with 2 ratings averaging 3.0.

Tab Blocker

Tab Blocker stops websites opening new tabs in Safari on the sites you switch it on for, and does nothing elsewhere. You turn it on from its button in Safari and reload the page. No servers, accounts or analytics (App Store).

Where it beats Undirect

It is free, runs on iOS 16, and has one switch per site with nothing else to learn. It is at version 1.0 from March 2026, with 6 ratings averaging 3.7.

Undirect

This is the one we make, a Safari extension for iPhone, iPad and Mac that stops a page taking your tap. On a site where you switch it on, it removes an invisible layer before your tap lands, puts back a link rewritten between the press and the release, and lets a new tab open only where your press pointed. A window opens when a site asks for one at a size and its address leads back to the page, the way a sign-in does. If a redirect rides your tap, the tab is sent back and the address is blocked in Safari's content blocking, so the next attempt never loads.

The popup lists every address the page tried, blocked or not, and you can allow or block each one on that site or everywhere. It costs $1.99 once, with no subscription or in-app purchase, and one purchase covers iPhone, iPad and Mac. It runs without an account, analytics or a server, and the source code is public.

Where the others beat it

It does not block ads, trackers or cookie notices. It costs money where the others are free to start. It needs iOS 26.2 or macOS 15, newer than any other entry here, and it is too new to have ratings. It judges a trip off the site by the tap behind it, so a page that redirects as it loads, with no tap at all, is left alone unless you have blocked that address. You can try it on the demo sites before you decide.

A name you may see that is not a blocker

RedirectWeb comes up in searches for a Safari redirect blocker. Its listing describes a URL router: it sends a link to a different address by rules you write, for example Google Maps links to Apple Maps. It does not say it blocks pop-ups or redirects (App Store).

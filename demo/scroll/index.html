Harbour reopens to small boats after a week of storms

Crews cleared the slipway by hand and by digger. The ferry is due back on Saturday, and repairs to the outer wall will run through the winter.

ME

Mara EllisonCoast correspondent

Updated 2 hours ago5 min read

The harbour master lifted the closure on Thursday morning, a week after the first of three storms pushed waves over the outer wall and left the slipway buried in shingle.

Crews worked through the weekend to clear it. By Wednesday evening the first fishing boats were tied up at the inner quay again, and the ferry is expected back on Saturday.

“We had a metre of stones where the slip should be,” said Ines Calloway, the harbour master. “You could not have launched a dinghy, never mind the lifeboat’s tender.”

The closure was the longest since the harbour office began keeping daily records. For seven days nothing larger than a kayak left the inner basin, and the fish market sold what it could bring in by road.

Three storms in seven days

The first storm arrived on the Thursday before last with a spring tide behind it. The gauge at the end of the outer wall measured waves of 4.1 metres that night, the highest of the week. Two more fronts followed, on Saturday and on Monday, before the wind dropped.

Most of the damage is to the seaward face of the wall, where the sea lifted out a row of coping stones and opened a gap about as long as a bus. Divers will survey the footings next week, once the water clears.

Repairs will take most of the winter. The council says the work will be done from the water side where it can, so the promenade stays open.

Traders count the cost

Traders on the front said the week cost them their best weekend since the summer, and asked for the car park charges to be lifted while the works go on.

“Nobody walks the front in a gale. Let people park for nothing until the wall is mended.”

Tomas Reyne, who runs the cafe by the slipway

The council’s harbour committee meets on Tuesday and will take the request then. A spokesperson said short-stay charges were “under review for the length of the repairs”.

At the fish market, stallholders were back at their usual pitches on Thursday. The first landing of the morning, mostly crab and a few boxes of pollack, sold out before nine.

What happens next

The lifeboat crew thanked the volunteers who checked on moorings through the worst of it. Eleven boats broke free or dragged their anchors during the week, and all but one were recovered without damage.

The ferry company said it would run a reduced timetable from Saturday and return to the full one once the pontoon has been inspected.

Weather for the week ahead is calmer, with light winds and sun by Sunday.

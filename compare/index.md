# Safari Block Pop-ups, content blockers and Undirect compared

Three different tools get called a pop-up blocker. Undirect is not an ad blocker. Facts below come from Apple's support pages and each product's own App Store listing, read on 7 November 2026 in the US store.

| Feature | Safari Block Pop-ups | [1Blocker](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/1blocker-ad-blocker/id1365531024) | [AdGuard](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/adguard-ad-blocker-for-safari/id1047223162) | Undirect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | A switch inside iOS | App with a Safari content blocker | App with a Safari content blocker and DNS filtering | App with a Safari extension |
| Price | Free, part of iOS | Free to download. Its listing puts custom rules behind Premium | Free to use. Its listing puts custom rules, advanced YouTube blocking and security filters behind Pro, with a 7-day trial | Paid app, $1.99 in the US store |
| What it goes after | Pop-up windows | Ads, trackers and page elements, by filter list | Banners, pop-ups, autoplay videos, ads and trackers, by filter list | Invisible click layers, rewritten links and windows or tabs a page opens that you did not ask for |
| Where you control it | One on/off switch in Settings | Allowlist a site from the Safari extension; rules and filters in the app | Allowlists, blocklists and your own rules in the app | Per destination, for one site or everywhere; the guard is off on a site until you switch it on |
| Shows what a page tried | Not in Apple's description | Not in its listing | Statistics of blocked ads and trackers | Lists every address a page tried to open, blocked or not |
| Devices | iPhone, iPad and Mac | iPhone, iPad and Mac, synced by iCloud; iOS 16 or later | iOS 15 or later | iPhone, iPad and Mac; iOS 26.2 or later |
| Privacy as the maker describes it | Part of iOS | Says it has no access to webpages and does not track you | Says no account is needed; open source | Runs without an account, analytics or a server |

## Where the others do more

1Blocker and AdGuard block ads, trackers and page clutter, which Undirect does not touch. If banners, video ads or tracking are what bother you, use one of them. Safari's own switch costs nothing and covers pop-up windows, so there is no reason to leave it off.

## What Undirect does not do

It does not hide ads, banners or cookie notices, and it does not block trackers. It works in Safari only, on the sites where you have switched it on, and it needs iOS 26.2 or later. It also costs money where the others have a free tier. You can run a content blocker beside it, though we have not tested every combination.

## Where it fits

Undirect is for the page that takes your tap: the layer you cannot see, the link that changes under your finger, the tab you did not open.

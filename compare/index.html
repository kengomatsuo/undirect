Undirect vs 1Blocker vs AdGuard

Three different kinds of tool get called a pop-up blocker, and Undirect is not an ad blocker. Facts below come from Apple's support pages and each product's own App Store listing, read on 20 November 2026.

Undirect vs 1Blocker

1Blocker is free with one blocking category on at a time, for example Block Ads. Premium turns on custom rules for blocking any address or hiding any page element, and costs $2.99 a month, $14.99 a year or $39.99 once.

The two work at different moments. 1Blocker hands Safari its lists before a page loads, and its listing says it does not modify web pages in any way. Undirect runs in the page and acts when you tap: it removes the layer that would catch the tap and checks that a new tab goes where you pressed. 1Blocker's listing says nothing about redirects or tabs opened with your tap, and it says "not all ads can be blocked" with Safari's content blocking. Undirect blocks no ads at all, so the two cover different things and can run together.

Undirect vs AdGuard

AdGuard Ad Blocker for Safari is free for Safari ad blocking, with Pro sold in the app; the listing shows purchases from $1.99 to $65.99. Its listing names pop-ups among the things it blocks, along with banners, autoplay videos, YouTube ads and trackers, and its DNS filtering reaches other apps too, which Undirect never does. On a Mac it is a separate free app, AdGuard Mini.

Undirect handles what a page does with your tap. It lists every address the page tried and lets you allow or block each one, on that site or everywhere. If you already run AdGuard and pages still open where you did not tap, that is the part Undirect covers.

Where the others do more

1Blocker and AdGuard block ads, trackers and page clutter, which Undirect does not touch. If banners, video ads or tracking are what bother you, use one of them. Safari's own switch costs nothing and covers pop-up windows, so there is no reason to leave it off.

What Undirect does not do

It does not hide ads, banners or cookie notices, and it does not block trackers. It works in Safari only, on the sites where you have switched it on, and it needs iOS 26.2 or later. It also costs money where the others have a free tier. You can run a content blocker beside it, though we have not tested every combination.

Where it fits

Undirect is for the page that takes your tap: the layer you cannot see, the link that changes under your finger, the tab you did not open.

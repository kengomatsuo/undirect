Free Safari ad blocking with custom filter lists and userscripts. 3.2.2 adds opt-in Global Privacy Control and safer iCloud sync. No subscription. wBlock is a free, open-source ad blocker for Safari on Mac, iPhone, iPad, and Apple Vision Pro. Block ads and trackers with up to 750,000 Safari content-blocking rules. No subscription or in-app purchases. Safari ad blocking Choose recommended filter lists or add your own by URL, text, or file. Regional lists cover websites in your language. Optional filters handle cookie banners and other annoyances. Lists update automatically, and you can inspect the rules each list produces. Userscripts and userstyles Run built-in scripts such as AdGuard Extra and Dark Reader, or import your own userscripts. UserCSS themes support plain CSS and USO, along with Less and Stylus. Return YouTube Dislike is available in the catalog. Native video controls Optional Tube Cleaner and Player Cleaner bring Safari's native video controls to supported players, with Picture-in-Picture and background audio. Tube Cleaner includes SponsorBlock settings for YouTube. DeArrow is available separately for alternative titles and thumbnails. Control each website Turn filtering or userscripts off for a site from Safari's toolbar popup. Use the Element Zapper to hide an unwanted overlay. Limit individual lists to selected websites or exclude sites, including domain patterns such as amazon.*. Pause blocking when you need to. Privacy settings Tracking-parameter removal cleans supported links. Opt in to Global Privacy Control to send websites your privacy preference. An optional toolbar count shows supported blocked requests; it is not a complete count of everything Safari blocks. iCloud sync and backups Sync filter lists and settings across your devices, including custom filters and userscripts. Export a local backup when you want one. On Mac, scheduled filter updates can run while the app is closed. Free and open source wBlock collects no data and requires no account. Its GPL-3.0 source code is available on GitHub. All features are free.

    I was a longtime user of AdGuard but eventually grew tired of the extra maintenance and troubleshooting it required. Add to that the growing list of paywalled features and the inconsistent experience across macOS, iOS, and iPadOS.My next stop was Wipr 2. I had used the original in the past and decided to give the new version a try. I actually don’t have much negative to say about it; it just didn’t fully meet my needs. For someone looking for a simple, reasonably effective “set it and forget it” ad blocker, I’d recommend it. Unfortunately, it wasn’t enough to eliminate many of the web nuisances I wanted gone.I then tried uBlock Origin Lite. Admittedly, if I hadn’t discovered wBlock, I probably would have stuck with it despite its occasional quirks. In particular, it seemed to periodically forget its settings.wBlock is similar to uBlock Origin Lite in many ways, but I find its UI and feature implementation far superior. It comes with an extensive set of filters out of the box, with even more available for those who want more aggressive blocking.In my testing, it was just as effective as AdGuard at blocking trackers, the best at blocking video ads, and had the best element-blocking tool (Zapper). Its filter sets are also extremely granular. In other words, it either matches or outperforms the others in virtually every area.And that’s without even mentioning the feature that ultimately sold me on it: userscripts. That’s a game changer for me. I won’t try to explain the feature here because the developers already do a good job of that, but for me, it was the missing piece I’d been looking for.Combine that with the superior design, and I plan to stick with wBlock as long as it continues to meet (and exceed) my expectations. And did I mention it’s free and open source?!My only complaint is that occasionally a custom filter or user script that I deleted keeps reappearing. I can’t tell if that’s a iCloud syncing issue or what but I have 1-2 that keeps respawning. It shows with a red “not downloaded” text.

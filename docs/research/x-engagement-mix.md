# What small Apple app makers post on X, counted: the mix that earns replies

Research for the owner's ask of 2026-11-22: "we are more like trying to gain engagement instead of
forcing every single product down their ears. Like, what idea for this? What would you like? Maybe a
small poll, and then any others? Comment down below or reply."

Method: each account's Posts tab was opened on x.com in Chrome, signed in as @monholtco, on
2026-11-22 (WIB), in one tab of its own, and scrolled until about 30 of the account's own posts had
loaded. Nothing was liked, followed, reposted or posted. A post here is one the account wrote
(original, quote, or a continuation of its own thread); reposts of other people and the quoted
source posts are left out. For each: kind, whether it names the account's own product, whether its
own text carries an outside link, and replies (r) and likes (l) as shown that day. URLs are
`https://x.com/<handle>/status/<id>`. Written account by account as each was read; the summary at
the end is counted from these tables.

Kinds: **product** (release, launch, feature, sale), **bip** (build in public: work in progress, a
number, a mistake, a decision), **tip** (something a stranger can use), **question** (asks the reader),
**poll**, **opinion** (a take on the trade or news), **personal** (life, jokes, memes), **others**
(praise or news of someone else's work).

What the product posts are. Naming the product is common; pitching it is not. Of the 124 maker posts
that name the product, 53 carry its link, and those are launches, releases and sale-count posts. The
other 71 are stories with the product as their subject: a tease clip, a number, a decision, a
mistake. @SebastianRoehl names his apps in 67% of posts and links the App Store in none.

What a pitch earns against everything else, inside the same account and the same week:
- @rashem48: bare link drop, median 1 reply; a sale told as a story 15; a question 9.
- @iPritamX: a post about his product, median 57 views; a question to other makers 1,456; an App Review story 4,441.
- @simonbs: product post with link, median 8.5 likes; the same products teased with no link 37; a gripe every Mac user shares 162 to 384.
- @PaulSolt: product line plus store link, 1 and 3 likes; a tip 263; "is it just me?" 12 replies.
- @dudufolio: feature posts average 4 replies; questions average 13.
- @jordibruin: questions 11 replies on 18 likes or fewer; four link-free teases 560 to 1,650 likes, then one launch post with the link.
- @Flighty and @AdGuard: sale posts sit at the bottom of their month by likes.
- The one exception is @FloWritesCode, whose native polls (2 replies) drew fewer replies than his product posts (median 4).

How polls are worded (4 native polls in 398 posts):
- One sentence, about the reader's own situation or a decision the maker is making that day: "Have you ever run one?", "How many devices in your home are connected to the internet?", "Should I release ASC CLI 7.0.0 today or tomorrow".
- Three of the four name no product; the fourth names it because the release is the question.
- Two to four options of one to four words, with one honest way out or a joke: "Wait, what's that?" (the winner, 40%), "I don't know", "Just gimme".
- Votes run 0.25% to 0.7% of followers (30, 39, 53 and 161 votes at 11.9K, 11.9K, 21.1K and 22.8K) and replies 0 to 7. A native poll collects taps. The poll written as text, @rashem48's "Mac users, where's your Dock? A) Bottom B) Left C) Right D) Hidden", drew 9 replies from 2K followers, more than any native poll here from accounts five to eleven times larger.
- @FloWritesCode follows a poll with a post that reads the result back and asks the next question.

How questions are worded (20 in 299):
- Answerable in a word or a number from the reader's own life: "what Mac app is missing here?" (36 replies), "what's the number that will make you quit your 9 to 5? Mine is $3K/month" (15), "is this a good average position?" over a screenshot (17).
- The asker often goes first with their own answer.
- "Are you seeing that, or is it just me?" after a complaint (12 replies on 18 likes).
- None names the asker's product. The two that failed (0 replies) were narrow hardware questions only a few readers could answer.

Where links go:
- In the bio, on all 14 accounts.
- In the post itself on launch, release and sale days only: 18% of maker posts, 8% of brand posts.
- As the product's bare domain used as its name mid-sentence ("Shotbase.com is based", "My side project tryfocuskit.com is ..."): @dudufolio, @SebastianRoehl, @rashem48.
- In the author's own reply under the post: @iPritamX (6 of 25 posts), @AdGuard (sale and blog links), @simonbs, @jordibruin, @FloWritesCode. Those replies are barely seen (14 to 23 views at @iPritamX, 302 at @AdGuard against 4,604 for the post above it), which is the point: the post travels, and the link is there for the few who look.
- 77% of maker posts and 91% of brand posts carry no link at all.

Voice. The two brand accounts with a maker's account beside them (@HabitKit, @macpiphq) get a
twentieth or less of the views the maker gets for the same product. The brand accounts that do earn
attention (@Flighty, @AdGuard) post news, numbers and explainers from their own field and keep the
product out of most of them. For @monholtco that means writing as the people who make the apps
("we", and "I" where one person did the thing), about the reader's own phone and browser, with the
apps as the subject of a story now and then.

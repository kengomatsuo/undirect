## What makes it read as real
- Not physics but the best-known film beat of a gust taking a sheet: the gust is shown by the sheet bending first (held corner, free edge lifting) before it lets go. The release is a beat, not a smooth ramp. Our first sheet could do the same: bend against the stone, then pop free.
- The held sheet bends in one big curve from the held point, with the far edge fluttering; no wrinkles.
- In black and white the sheets read only by value: bright face toward the light, dark face away. A good test for our sheets: they should read in greyscale.
- Camera: the gust is sold by hair, coat and paper moving the same way at the same time.

## How it was made
The technology video says the short began as ordinary 3D CG animation, then artists drew lines in 2D that a system moved through time with vector fields from the 3D animation. The page does not say whether the paper itself was keyframed or simulated (UNSURE).

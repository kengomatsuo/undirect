# 02 Just For Laughs Gags: "Paper Gone With The Wind"

- URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T_teQMbpEKs
- Creator: Just For Laughs Gags (YouTube, uploaded 2011, 192K views when opened)
- Kind: real footage, real time (about 30 fps, not slow motion). A truck-mounted giant fan blasts stacks of loose A4 sheets off a picnic table.
- Frames: `frames/f01_t7.5.jpg` to `f04_t8.25.jpg` (the first gust, 0.25 s apart), `f05_t32.0.jpg`, `f06_t33.0.jpg` (a person pinning a stack while the top sheets peel), `f07_t42.0.jpg` wide, `f08_t43.0.jpg` the stack close-up on the table. Page: `page.jpg`, `page.txt`.

## What makes it read as real
- The nearest thing found to our shot: stacks held down (by hands and arms instead of a stone) while a gust peels sheets off the top. Sheets leave one or a few at a time from the top and the windward edge; the stack below stays put.
- Speed: in real time the sheets cross the frame in about a second and change orientation visibly between frames 0.25 s apart (estimate from the frames, UNSURE as a number). Our film should not be slower than this unless it is clearly slow motion.
- Path: sheets stay low and skim, bounce off the grass and lift again; they do not rise in clean arcs. Many travel edge-first.
- Bend: most sheets stay nearly flat with a slight bow or a bent corner; only the sheets still trapped under a hand fold hard. A free sheet in a strong wind does not crumple.
- Light: hard sun; a sheet flips from bright white to mid grey as it turns away, which gives the flicker that reads as tumbling.
- Count: dozens at once in the wide shots, so no single sheet is studied; the eye reads the mass.
- Camera: handheld TV coverage, wide and close cuts.

## How it was made
Practical prank with a real fan (page description). No breakdown.

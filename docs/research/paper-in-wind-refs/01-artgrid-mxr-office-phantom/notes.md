## What makes it read as real
- Dozens of A4 sheets thrown up in an office, all slowed so a sheet takes several seconds to turn once. Turn rate is slow and uneven: a sheet hangs nearly still, then flips its edge to camera in a fraction of the clip.
- Bend: almost every sheet holds one soft cylindrical bow (a single curve across the short side), sometimes a curled corner. No sheet ripples like cloth; the paper's stiffness keeps big flat areas.
- Edge-on sheets collapse to a thin bright line, which is what sells the thinness. Our sheets should spend real time edge-on.
- Light: sheets near the window go translucent and glow; printed sides show through from behind (backlight through 80 g paper). Shadow side is a soft grey, not black.
- Count and depth: near sheets fill a third of frame and blur heavily (shallow focus), mid sheets sharp, far sheets small. Depth layering does more for realism than any single sheet.
- Camera: locked, eye level, long lens; the subject is still and the paper does all the moving.

Practical: thrown paper shot at high frame rate on a Phantom Flex 4K (camera named on the page). No breakdown found.

How to stop Safari redirects and pop-ups on iPhone

You tapped something on a page and landed on a site you never chose. Here is what happened and what you can switch on.

What a redirect or pop-under is

A redirect sends the tab you are reading to another address, often a betting, dating or fake virus-warning page. A pop-under opens a second page behind the one you are reading, so you only find it when you close the first.

Both usually start with your tap. The page lays a transparent layer over its content and takes the press, or it changes where a link points between the moment your finger lands and the moment it lifts. You aimed at the article and the page's advertiser got the click.

Turn on Safari's Block Pop-ups

Open Settings, tap Apps, tap Safari, and switch on Block Pop-ups. That is the path in Apple's iPhone guide for iOS 26, and its iOS 18 edition gives the same steps. Apple's article on pop-ups also warns that some of them draw a fake close button, so when in doubt close the tab instead of tapping the X. Sources: iPhone User Guide and Apple Support article 102524.

It is free, it is already on your phone, and you should turn it on.

What that setting does not stop

Apple describes the switch in a few lines and leaves its limits unlisted. These four things are not pop-up windows opened by a script, which is why a phone with Block Pop-ups on can still end up somewhere you did not choose:

  • A page that sends the tab you are reading to a different address.
  • An invisible layer that catches your tap and opens something with it.
  • A link that is rewritten between the press and the release.
  • A blank tab opened in the background while the one you were reading is pointed somewhere else.

Undirect covers that part

Undirect is a Safari extension for iPhone, iPad and Mac. It removes the invisible layer before your tap lands, puts a rewritten link back, and lets a new window open only when a site asks for one the way a sign-in does. Every address a page tried to send you to is listed, blocked or not, and you can allow or block each one for that site or everywhere.

It is off until you press its toolbar button on a site, and it runs without an account, analytics or a server. It needs iOS 26.2 or later. It costs $1.99 in the US store.

If you are unsure, see how it differs from Safari's setting and from content blockers, or try it on the made-up demo sites.

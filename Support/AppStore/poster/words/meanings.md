# What each line of Undirect's store frames says

Undirect is a Safari extension for iPhone, iPad and Mac. It stops a web page from taking your tap or click to open
a pop-up, a new tab or a redirect you did not ask for. It is turned on for a site by pressing its row in Safari's
page menu (on Mac, its toolbar button). It lists every address a page tried to send you to, and each can be blocked
or allowed on one site or everywhere. It also stops the trick where pressing Back sends you somewhere else instead of
the page you came from. No account, no analytics, no server; nothing leaves the device. One purchase covers iPhone,
iPad and Mac.

The frames are a poster: a giant headline of two to five words per frame, read at thumbnail size, a short line under
it. Headlines are blunt, everyday speech, the result for the buyer, never a feature list or a technical word. "|" is
the line break in a headline (two short lines; each line at most about 12 characters in Latin script, so it can be set
huge). {name} is the app's name as that language writes it.

| key | Frame shows | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1.h | Pop-up ad windows bursting out over a video site | You tap play on a video and a pop-up opens instead. The everyday annoyance, said as two quick beats: you do this, you get that. |
| 1.s | | And then another tab opens, and another: it keeps happening. |
| 2.h | Safari's page menu with {name}'s row being pressed | One press and it stops. |
| 2.s | | Turn on {name} from Safari's page menu (the menu in the address bar). |
| 3.h | The video page, the video playing, a small "Stopped" note at the bottom | Your tap stays yours: it does what you meant. |
| 3.s | | The video plays. No new tab opens. |
| 4.h | Undirect's list of what the page reached for, each row Blocked | Every catch (every trick it caught), listed. |
| 4.s | | See what each page tried to open. |
| 5.h | A "Back button trap stopped" note | Back means back: pressing Back takes you back. |
| 5.s | | The Back button takes you back to where you were. Write "Back button" in full, the name people use for it. |
| 6.h | The rules list: blocked and allowed sites | Your rules, site by site. |
| 6.s | | Block something on one site, allow it on another. |
| 7.h | Settings | Nothing leaves (your device). Privacy, said in two or three words. |
| 7.s | | No account, no tracking, no server. |
| 8.h | iPad, iPhone and Mac together | iPhone, iPad and Mac. Device names stay as Apple writes them in that language. |
| 8.s | | One purchase covers all three. |
| 8.f | | No account, no subscription. |
| ads | Six fake ad buttons on the pop-ups | The shouty words scammy pop-up ads use in that language: "YOU WON!", "CLICK HERE", "DOWNLOAD", "FREE SPINS", "HOT DEAL", "CLAIM NOW". Short, capitals where the script has them. |
| film.title | The made-up film on the video site | A made-up film title, two or three words, natural in that language (English: "Night Harbor", a ferry story). |
| film.meta | Under the title | year · length · genre, the way a streaming site writes it in that language: "2026 · 1 h 48 min · Thriller". |
| site_nav | The site's menu | Four menu words of a streaming site: Films, Series, New, My list. |
| 2m.s | Mac frame 2: Safari on the Mac, Undirect's toolbar button pressed | The Mac's version of 2.s: turn on {name} with its button in Safari's toolbar. |
